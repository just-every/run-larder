# Larder product contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Larder is the manufacturing ERP/MRP a US small discrete manufacturer or maker can switch to from Katana, MRPeasy, Fishbowl Manufacturing, Odoo Manufacturing, Fulcrum or DOSS. It turns demand into a feasible material and capacity plan, executes shop-floor work, traces lots and quality, costs actual production, ships orders and reconciles connected books.

This is the product we are building towards. A missing capability is engineering work, not a reduced promise. Each named clause states buyer behaviour and acceptance evidence through a public screen, API, command, download or provider connection. The final Status table measures current code against the whole destination; [ACCEPTANCE.md](ACCEPTANCE.md) lists the same journeys and statuses. Named acceptance journeys for missing work are requirements, not claims that tests already exist.

## Product target

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Category | Manufacturing ERP / MRP for small manufacturers and makers |
| Incumbents a buyer switches from | Katana, MRPeasy, Fishbowl Manufacturing, Odoo Manufacturing, Fulcrum, DOSS |
| First buyer | A US owner or operations manager of a 5–50-person discrete manufacturer/maker, selling made goods in USD, with shop-floor, warehouse, purchasing and accounts staff and QuickBooks Online or Xero as the authoritative external ledger |

The whole job includes engineering records, quotes and orders, make-to-stock/order, multilevel MRP, finite machine/labour scheduling, purchasing/subcontracting, tablet/barcode execution, stock and genealogy, food/cosmetic/supplement batch expiry and recall records, actual job/WIP costs, reporting, commerce and shipping, role controls, migration and recovery. Agents maintain approved plans and follow consequences through actual production and books. Operational quality/traceability is part of the product; qualified people remain responsible for release and regulatory decisions.

The sixteen published clauses keep their IDs and useful operational guarantees. The accountant CSV handoff stays a compatibility/export flow inside connected accounting; it is no longer the destination. Australian/AUD defaults and prior exclusions of capacity, integrations, barcode, serials, native apps and model calls are superseded by the target clauses. New US settings require an explicit migration of existing currency/identity data, never relabelling AUD amounts as USD.

## Operating rules

- Start empty. No sample stock, customers, synthetic provider outcomes or fallback balances enter a live business.
- Stock truth is append-only signed movements by item, location, status and lot/serial. Availability separates on-hand, reservations, quarantine, transit and expected supply. Database guards prevent negative positions and overselling regardless of caller.
- Exact fixed-point quantities/money use up to six decimal places; document/ledger rounding rules are explicit and versioned. Conversions must be defined and exact. Used units are immutable; changing their meaning needs a new SKU.
- Saved BOM revisions are immutable; released work pins BOM/routing/instruction/cost assumptions. Corrections are linked compensating movements; downstream dependencies block reversal until resolved.
- Every write is atomic and rechecks live native session, current role and state version. Stable command identities replay recorded outcomes; changed content under an identity is refused. New adapters use these same doors and invariants.
- Hard reservations do not silently expire. Reallocation, changed promises, purchasing, financial posting and quality disposition require the right recorded authority.
- Agents prepare; authorized people approve exact versioned inputs and external consequences. Untrusted enquiries, supplier files and drawings are evidence, never instructions. Deterministic arithmetic and ledger/physical evidence decide quantities and amounts.
- Provider app registration, OAuth review, carrier/marketplace access, model configuration and regulated deployment agreements are delivery work. Each connection must prove permitted provider readback; local test doubles at paid/network edges do not establish live acceptance.


## Published operational foundation

- **LARDER-001 — Items, variants, units and parties.** A person creates items (made or bought), variants of a template with distinct attribute sets, exact unit conversions, customers and suppliers, and each party's own code for an item. Ambiguous conversions, duplicate SKUs or variants, ambiguous party codes and duplicate party names are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Through authenticated HTTP create two variants, exact unit conversions and supplier/customer codes; refuse duplicate SKUs, variants, ambiguous codes, undefined or rounding conversions, and edits to used units.

- **LARDER-002 — Opening stock.** A person records a counted opening position by location with its count date, valuation basis (supplier invoices, last purchase, standard cost or owner estimate), unit cost, optional lot and a required evidence file kept with its digest. Opening stock cannot be posted twice for an item and location. **Acceptance evidence:** Record a counted, costed opening with its evidence attachment; download that attachment, reconcile the position, and refuse a second opening for the same item/location.

- **LARDER-003 — Versioned BOM and routing.** A person saves BOM revisions (components per finished unit, simple routing steps). Releasing work pins the active revision; a later revision does not change released work. **Acceptance evidence:** Release work on revision 1, save revision 2 and release another job; the first job keeps revision 1 and its original components/routing.

- **LARDER-004 — Quote and confirm.** A person quotes an order and confirms it. For every item Larder shows on hand, reserved, quarantine, expected from purchase, expected from production, unreserved demand and available to promise, each kept separate. **Acceptance evidence:** Quote and confirm an order through the order screen/API; inspect on-hand, reserved, quarantine, expected purchase/production and unreserved demand as separate figures.

- **LARDER-005 — Atomic reservation.** Confirming or reserving takes only available stock. Concurrent orders cannot both consume the same stock; a retried command returns its first outcome. **Acceptance evidence:** Race six HTTP reservations for the same stock; total reservations never exceed stock, an identical command replays its first outcome and changed content under its identity is refused.

- **LARDER-006 — Shortages.** Larder computes shortages deterministically from confirmed orders and released work against free stock and open supply, with the date needed and the documents that need it, and drafts a purchase order (preferred supplier and price) or a work order with that required-by date and reason. **Acceptance evidence:** Confirm an order exceeding free stock, draft its work shortage, release work, and draft the resulting purchased-component shortage; quantities, required dates and source documents agree.

- **LARDER-007 — Purchase approval.** A buyer or manager approves a draft purchase order (approval recorded with the exact terms and cash exposure), records the supplier's confirmation and reference, and revises the expected delivery with history. **Acceptance evidence:** Draft and approve a PO, confirm supplier reference/date, then revise its ETA; read exact approved terms, cash exposure and every date revision.

- **LARDER-008 — Partial receipt, lots and quarantine.** A warehouse person receives part of a purchase order into a location, records supplier lots and either accepts or holds stock in quarantine; a recorded release or rejection decision moves it on. Over-receipt is refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Part-receive a PO with supplier lot into quarantine; release part and reject part with reasons; refuse over-receipt and prevent held stock from reservation/picking.

- **LARDER-009 — Production.** Released work reserves its components; materials are issued as actually used (drawn across lots when no lot is named), good output and scrap are recorded against the pinned revision, and closing records material cost and variance. **Acceptance evidence:** Release work, issue actual components across lots, record good output and scrap, and close; inspect pinned BOM, cost and material variance through the API and work-order screen.

- **LARDER-010 — Pick, pack and partial shipment.** A warehouse person picks only reserved stock, packs, and ships part of an order; shipping consumes those reservations, and the remaining commitments stay reserved or short. **Acceptance evidence:** Reserve an order, pick only reserved stock, pack and partly ship; read remaining reservations/shortages and refuse an unreserved pick through HTTP.

- **LARDER-011 — Returns, transfers, counts and corrections.** Returns of shipped quantities come back to stock or quarantine; transfers move only free stock; counts by one person need another's approval before an adjustment (the owner, who holds every authority, may approve their own count so a one-person workshop can keep its stock right); a stale count is refused; reversals restore stock and order state through compensating movements and are refused while downstream work depends on the stock. **Acceptance evidence:** Receive a return, transfer free stock and approve another person's count; reject stale counts and downstream-dependent reversals; reverse shipment by compensating movements and retain its original.

- **LARDER-012 — Accountant handoff.** A manager or accounts person prepares an immutable batch of not-yet-handed-off movements, valued: supplier receipts, customer shipments for invoice preparation (with the order price), production issue and output, adjustments, returns and corrections. The batch downloads as CSV. Acknowledgements record ledger references line by line; differing amounts show as mismatches until corrected; a later reversal appears in the next batch as a correction pointing to the original batch and line. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare/download valued handoff CSV, acknowledge ledger references with a mismatch, correct it, reverse an earlier shipment and see its linked correction in the next batch.

- **LARDER-013 — Exceptions and the complete archive.** One queue shows shortages, overdue purchases, quarantine, waiting approvals and proposals, unreconciled handoffs and rejected move-in rows; a manager resolves the ones that are decisions, with a recorded note. The owner or a manager exports the complete business archive with a checksum; the owner restores it into an empty installation in one atomic write that proves every stock position against the archive. **Acceptance evidence:** Resolve a decision exception with a note; export/archive and restore into an empty installation; refuse a damaged archive and prove every restored position; round-trip an installation backup after migration/reset.

- **LARDER-014 — Authority everywhere.** Every command and read checks the current session and role in the handler and again inside the atomic write; losing a role or signing out takes effect on the next request; cross-site commands are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Call every protected door as signed out, viewer and restricted roles; revoke a role and sign out, then reuse the old cookie; cross-site commands and writes with stale live authority leave no mutation.

- **LARDER-015 — Agents prepare, people approve.** Through the same public command API an agent prepares: order intake from a pasted enquiry (kept as data, never obeyed; missing quantities block approval), replenishment as grouped purchase orders over deterministic shortages with its explanation and cash exposure, a duplicate-item merge with its previewed impact (stock moved by compensating movements), a work-order reschedule with conflicts, and a ledger reconciliation. A person with the right role, never the preparer (except the owner, who holds every authority) and never an agent, approves or declines the exact version; stale inputs are refused; every decision is recorded. **Acceptance evidence:** As an agent prepare intake, replenishment, merge, reschedule and reconciliation proposals; refuse incomplete/stale inputs and agent/self approval, then approve/decline exact versions as authorized people and inspect decisions.

- **LARDER-016 — Odoo kit.** A manager (or an agent, for preparation) previews Odoo import-compatible CSV exports of contacts, units, products and variants, locations, BOMs with routing, on-hand quantities and open sales and purchase orders. The preview is a dry run listing counts, rejected rows with file, line, External ID and reason, deliberate skips and unread columns. Committing publishes through Base's import kit, which journals the job and keeps every original file and digest. Undo before live use removes the whole batch; once later work depends on it, undo is refused and points at the dependency. The documented shape is in [MOVE-IN.md](MOVE-IN.md). **Acceptance evidence:** Upload Odoo import-compatible CSV via move-in, inspect file/line/External-ID errors, skips/unread columns, commit, inspect retained originals/digests and undo; malformed/replayed inputs and undo after live dependencies are refused.


## Target clauses

### US operating setup

- **US-001 — US business and USD defaults.** A US manufacturer sets legal name, US billing/shipping addresses, EIN reference, USD functional currency, US time zone, working week and fiscal year. Quantities support exact US customary and metric units; customer/supplier tax identities are distinct from Australian ABNs. Money and archives retain their declared currency. **Acceptance evidence:** Set up an Ohio workshop in USD, quote in inches and receive in feet with an exact conversion; export and restore the order and verify local dates, currency and identity. Reject an inexact conversion.

- **US-002 — Sales tax and exemption handoff.** Manufacturing quotes/orders carry ship-to jurisdiction, taxable items, freight, exemption/resale certificates and provider-calculated US sales tax via Avalara AvaTax or TaxJar. Final invoice tax goes to the connected ledger once; expiry or provider failure blocks an unsupported tax assertion. Larder retains the calculation and reversal references. **Acceptance evidence:** Quote an interstate taxable order and a resale-exempt order through the UI with a tax sandbox; ship/return part and reconcile invoice and tax reversal in QuickBooks Online.

- **US-003 — Guided real-order setup.** Setup guides the owner through units, locations, suppliers, BOMs, calendars, costing and a real order to shipment, showing missing prerequisites and understandable migration diagnostics. It starts empty, and progress resumes without a consulting-only configuration path. **Acceptance evidence:** In an empty installation complete setup using an actual workshop order; interrupt after BOM setup, resume and reach a feasible plan, production, shipment and reconciled ledger entry.


### Product engineering

- **ENG-001 — Multilevel BOM explosion.** Engineers build multilevel assemblies and phantom subassemblies with per-unit quantities, yields, effective revisions and make/buy choices. MRP recursively explodes them, detects cycles, and pegs component demand without double counting shared subassemblies. Released work remains pinned. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a three-level BOM sharing a component, stock one subassembly, run MRP and inspect net component demand; reject a cycle and preserve released work after revision.

- **ENG-002 — Executable routings and instructions.** A revision includes ordered operations, dependencies, setup/run/queue times, work-centre eligibility, labour skills, drawings and step instructions. Operators see the exact approved revision at their task, including a required acknowledgement after an instruction change. **Acceptance evidence:** Release a two-operation job, open its tablet task and drawing, then revise instructions; the released job retains its instructions and a new job requires acknowledgement.

- **ENG-003 — Configurable and matrix BOMs.** Sales/engineering choose permitted variant attributes, dimensions and options; rules derive component quantities, routing and cost with explainable formulas. Invalid combinations are refused and the resolved configuration is pinned on quote and work. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure two sizes/finishes through the quote form, compare resolved BOM/cost and release both; refuse an invalid combination and keep old work stable after a rule edit.

- **ENG-004 — BOM CSV export and bulk revision.** Engineers export BOMs/routings as CSV and preview batch component substitutions, quantity changes and effective dates with affected SKUs, stock and open-work impact. Approval creates revisions atomically; released work changes only by a separately reviewed change. **Acceptance evidence:** Export ten BOMs, replace one component through CSV preview, inspect row errors and affected work, approve and re-export; a released job keeps its previous revision.

- **ENG-005 — Engineering change approval and obsolescence.** Engineers propose changes with reason, effectivity, attachments, substitute eligibility and disposition of obsolete stock; authorized managers approve, record acknowledgement and choose treatment of unreleased work. Approved revision history is immutable. **Acceptance evidence:** Propose a safety-related component replacement, deny release before approval, approve on an effective date and inspect old stock disposition and unchanged released jobs.

- **ENG-006 — Kits and sale-triggered assembly.** Sellable kits have versioned component definitions; an authorized sale can trigger policy-approved assembly or component picking. Stock, kit availability and costs reconcile once even when the channel repeats the sale event. **Acceptance evidence:** Sell a kit twice via one replayed channel event and a separate second sale; verify two assemblies only, exact component consumption and no oversell.


### Demand and sales

- **DEMAND-001 — Costed quotes and credible promise dates.** Sales prepares versioned USD quotes including customer-specific prices, quantity breaks, discounts, freight, quote validity and estimated material/labour/overhead margin. Available-to-promise and capable-to-promise dates use dated supply and finite material/labour capacity, exposing assumptions and alternatives. **Acceptance evidence:** Quote a job with scarce material and a busy machine, compare immediate/partial/overtime options and their margin/date, accept one and trace the date to booked supply/capacity.

- **DEMAND-002 — Order changes and cancellations.** Sales versions order quantities, dates, addresses and prices with customer approval history. Changes identify affected reservations, procurement, work and shipped balances; cancellation releases only unconsumed commitments and leaves already incurred costs visible. **Acceptance evidence:** Increase, split-date and cancel the unshipped remainder of a partly produced order; inspect reservation/supply changes, retained shipment and incurred cost without deleting history.

- **DEMAND-003 — Master production schedule and forecasts.** Planners maintain time-bucketed sales forecasts and a master production schedule, import forecast CSV, distinguish actual orders from forecast consumption, and record overrides, seasonality and forecast error. Actual demand consumes forecasts rather than doubling them. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a 12-week forecast, confirm overlapping sales orders and release an MPS; compare net demand and forecast error by week, then undo an override with history.

- **DEMAND-004 — Make-to-stock and make-to-order policies.** Each SKU/location selects make-to-stock, make-to-order or assemble-to-order with reorder point, safety stock, min/max, batch size, order multiple and lead time. Customer-specific work stays pegged while common stock supplies approved competing demand. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the same demand with stock and order policies, approve the suggested batches and inspect customer pegging, safety stock and unused common inventory.

- **DEMAND-005 — Allocation priority and customer commitments.** Planners allocate scarce supply by customer/service priority with due-date and margin consequences. Reallocation shows displaced orders, requires approval and preserves hard-reservation integrity; customers receive only approved promise changes. **Acceptance evidence:** Allocate one receipt between two orders, preview displacement, approve a split and inspect both promises and reservation totals; deny a sales user authority to steal stock.


### Material planning

- **MRP-001 — Time-phased demand netting.** MRP nets dated sales, forecast, dependent BOM demand and safety stock against usable inventory, reservations, yields, open POs, WIP and lead times. Late, held or expiring supply cannot cover earlier demand. Each suggestion explains quantity, date and sources. **Acceptance evidence:** Place demand before and after a supplier ETA with held/expiring stock and multilevel WIP; run MRP through the planning API and verify every dated shortage and proposed release/receipt.

- **MRP-002 — Demand pegging and supply changes.** A planner follows any component, receipt or work order to the customer demand it supplies and reverse-pegs all affected orders. Cancelled/late/excess supply produces expedite, defer or cancel recommendations with impact and approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Delay a component PO and cancel another demand; open pegging and approve one expedite and one cancellation, verifying affected orders and no orphaned reservation.

- **MRP-003 — Replenishment forecasts and purchasing constraints.** Planners review demand history, forecast confidence, supplier calendars, MOQs, packs, price breaks and lead-time variability to set safety stock and suggested orders. The buyer sees coverage and USD cash exposure before converting suggestions. **Acceptance evidence:** Change a vendor MOQ and uncertain lead time, run replenishment and inspect rounded quantities, service coverage and cash; approve only selected suggestions and retain rejected reasons.

- **MRP-004 — Planning scenarios and release fence.** Planners compare baseline and scenarios for demand, inventory, overtime, alternate suppliers and equipment outages without mutating live commitments. A frozen horizon protects released work; adoption revalidates every changed input and books the approved plan. **Acceptance evidence:** Compare two outage scenarios, freeze this week and adopt next week's plan; change an ETA before adoption and see stale approval refused without a partial schedule.


### Capacity planning

- **CAP-001 — Work-centre calendars.** Planners define machines/work centres, parallel capacity, shifts, breaks, US holidays, maintenance, efficiency and downtime exceptions with time zone and effective dates. Planned operations occupy actual available intervals. **Acceptance evidence:** Schedule across a US holiday and overnight shift, add a machine outage and verify no job occupies unavailable machine capacity.

- **CAP-002 — Labour calendars and skills.** Supervisors schedule employees/teams by shift, availability, time off, skills and simultaneous-task limits, independent of machine availability. Wage/burden rates are restricted to accounts/authorized managers; time capture feeds costing rather than payroll. **Acceptance evidence:** Give two machines one qualified operator, book vacation and run scheduling; inspect feasible assignments and deny operator access to wage rates.

- **CAP-003 — Operation dependencies and blocked work.** Operations obey predecessor completion, transfer batches, overlap rules, material availability and quality gates. A blocked task shows its exact reason and owner, and becomes dispatchable only after the dependency is satisfied. **Acceptance evidence:** Try starting coating before machining or QC release through tablet/API; inspect refusal, complete the dependency and start without bypassing it.

- **CAP-004 — Finite-capacity Gantt and bottlenecks.** A production Gantt and load view place jobs in finite machine and labour capacity with due dates, setup/run durations, slack, critical path and bottlenecks. Unscheduled/unfeasible jobs remain explicit; the planner can trace each promised date to scheduled operations. **Acceptance evidence:** Schedule competing multistep orders on constrained machine/labour calendars; inspect Gantt/load/critical path and infeasible work, with no simultaneous use beyond capacity.

- **CAP-005 — Drag and drop rescheduling.** Planners drag jobs or operations in the Gantt or use keyboard/date controls; preview downstream moves, displaced orders, capacity conflicts and promise changes, then approve a versioned schedule. A conflict cannot silently overload capacity. **Acceptance evidence:** Move an urgent job ahead of another using mouse and keyboard; compare preview and approved sequence, and reject a stale change after a shop-floor update.

- **CAP-006 — Alternate centres and sequence-dependent setups.** Scheduling chooses qualified alternate work centres and tools, accounts for setup families, changeover/cleanup times and minimum transfer quantities, and compares outsourcing/overtime where allowed. A chosen alternate preserves required quality/instructions. **Acceptance evidence:** Remove a primary machine, compare two eligible alternates with different changeovers, approve one and verify setup, labour, delivery and cost consequences.

- **CAP-007 — Live dependency-preserving reflow.** Progress, material ETA, breakdown and priority events trigger a proposed reflow preserving dependency buffers, frozen work and human overrides. Under an explicit bounded policy the planner can authorize automatic internal sequencing; external dates/spend always require their recorded authority. **Acceptance evidence:** Stop a machine mid-job, restart the scheduler and inspect one reflow; preserve the frozen horizon and manual lock, refuse an unapproved customer promise change and show displaced work.

- **CAP-008 — Grouped jobs and nesting.** Planners group compatible operations/batches and import nesting results with material yield, offcuts, setup sharing and per-job quantity/cost allocation. Groups retain each customer/job identity, required revision and due date. **Acceptance evidence:** Group three sheet-metal jobs, import a nesting output, approve consumption and recoverable offcuts; trace each output and allocated cost to its original job.

- **CAP-009 — Maintenance and downtime planning.** Supervisors schedule preventive maintenance and log unplanned breakdowns with owner, expected return and repair evidence; equipment stays unavailable until authorized release. Maintenance changes feed production recovery, while specialist CMMS records can link by ID. **Acceptance evidence:** Take a machine out of service, assign repair and ETA, reschedule affected work, then release after recorded repair and inspect the plan and downtime trail.


### Purchasing and suppliers

- **BUY-001 — Supplier contracts and delivery rules.** Buyers maintain supplier/item codes, prices/breaks, currency, payment/freight terms, lead times, MOQs, calendars, preferred/alternate vendors and performance history. POs snapshot terms, USD exposure and delivery schedules; acknowledgements preserve supplier revisions. **Acceptance evidence:** Create two vendors with different packs/lead times, select a feasible quote, approve a PO and revise the supplier date; old approved terms remain recoverable.

- **BUY-002 — Receiving against production demand.** Receivers handle partial deliveries, tolerances, damage, supplier documents, lots and inspection, then allocate accepted receipts to pegged work and sales commitments. A short delivery updates the remaining PO and downstream plan. **Acceptance evidence:** Receive a short delivery for two pegged jobs, quarantine damaged units and allocate the accepted quantity; inspect open PO, work shortages and proposed date changes.

- **BUY-003 — Purchasing approvals and three-way match.** Amount/category rules route PO and change approvals to authorized people with separation of duties. Supplier bills match PO, accepted receipts, freight and tax; quantity/price/currency differences wait for accounts approval before ledger posting. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve a PO below and above a threshold, upload a mismatching supplier invoice and part receipt; resolve the variance, post one bill and deny self-approval under the configured rule.

- **BUY-004 — Subcontract and free-issue production.** Buyers issue components to subcontractors, track stock at their location, place subcontract operations/POs and receive finished work with service costs, yield and lot links. Co-manufacturing tracks customer-owned materials separately without valuing them as owned inventory. **Acceptance evidence:** Send two material lots to an outside finisher, part-receive finished goods and scrap, reconcile remaining free-issue stock and service bill, and trace the finished lot.

- **BUY-005 — Landed costs.** Accounts allocates freight, duty, insurance and handling across receipts by explicit weight/value/quantity policy, supports later supplier charges and approved cost corrections, and reconciles stock, consumed COGS and WIP without rewriting prior movements. **Acceptance evidence:** Allocate a late freight bill across partly consumed receipts, review stock/COGS/WIP adjustments and send approved correcting journals to the ledger.

- **BUY-006 — Supplier returns and credits.** Receivers initiate supplier RMAs for rejected or excess material with lot/serial/quantity evidence, ship only eligible stock and match replacement or vendor credit to the original receipt and bill. Open claims have owner and due date. **Acceptance evidence:** Reject a received lot, ship a partial supplier return, receive a replacement and match the remaining credit; verify stock, trace and purchasing/accounting balances.

- **BUY-007 — Supplier and customer communications.** Larder sends approved POs, acknowledgements, RFQs, supplier ETA chases and customer promise notices by Base mail through Microsoft 365/Gmail or the configured delivery provider. Threaded inbound replies and attachments are untrusted evidence, with delivery/failure/unknown outcomes and visible follow-up. **Acceptance evidence:** Approve/send a PO, receive an ETA reply, preview its plan impact and approve an updated promise notice; a timeout remains uncertain and never becomes a false confirmation.


### Shop-floor execution

- **FLOOR-001 — Tablet and phone dispatch.** An operator sees assigned ready/blocked operations, priority, drawings, BOM revision, quantities and safety/quality checks on a shop-floor tablet or phone. Start, pause, resume and complete record named operator, station and reason; gloves/large targets and shared-station reauthentication are supported. **Acceptance evidence:** At 1440 and 390 widths dispatch two real jobs, attempt blocked work, sign out a shared station and complete one operation; task state survives reload and another operator sees the update.

- **FLOOR-002 — Actual labour and operation time.** Operators clock setup/run/cleanup time, parallel attendance and pauses against operations. Supervisors approve corrections with reason; overlapping/impossible entries are flagged, and locked periods need accounts approval to reopen. Costing uses effective authorized rates. **Acceptance evidence:** Clock a setup and run, pause for breakdown, correct a missed stop and approve it; verify cost and audit while a duplicate tap and overlapping shift cannot double labour.

- **FLOOR-003 — WIP and manufacturing backorders.** Supervisors see quantities and actual costs waiting, running, completed, held and scrapped by operation. Partial completion transfers only permitted quantity to downstream work, and uncompleted demand remains a dated manufacturing backorder with material/capacity commitments. **Acceptance evidence:** Complete six of ten units at operation one, transfer four downstream, hold two and backorder four; inspect WIP, reservations and remaining dates/costs.

- **FLOOR-004 — Rework repair and unbuild.** Dedicated rework/unbuild orders cite original lot/job, disposition and authorized instructions, recover usable components, capture extra material/labour and segregate rejected stock. Rework never creates free inventory or loses genealogy. **Acceptance evidence:** Return a defective assembly, unbuild recoverable parts, rework the rest and ship an approved repair; trace extra cost, recovered stock and original lot.

- **FLOOR-005 — Split and merge manufacturing orders.** Planners split or merge compatible unreleased/released work under explicit material/WIP rules, retaining original demand, lots, recorded time and costs. Incompatible revisions, already shipped outputs and duplicate quantities block merging. **Acceptance evidence:** Split a partly completed job for two centres, merge compatible remaining work, then try incompatible revisions; quantities, time and source-order links reconcile.

- **FLOOR-006 — Co-products and by-products.** A manufacturing order records multiple planned outputs, by-products and recoverable scrap with exact yield and approved cost-allocation policy. Each output has its own lot, quality disposition and stock destination. **Acceptance evidence:** Produce two co-products and a reusable by-product from one batch, inspect lot links and cost allocations, and verify total output value equals approved actual cost.

- **FLOOR-007 — Downtime reasons and production progress.** Operators report breakdown, waiting material, changeover, quality and other lost-time reasons with start/stop and escalation owner. Supervisors see live operation progress and revised remaining effort; reported delay updates planning without inventing completion. **Acceptance evidence:** Pause a running operation for a broken tool, notify its supervisor, revise remaining effort and inspect lost-time report and the affected plan dates.

- **FLOOR-008 — Barcode scanning workflows.** USB/Bluetooth scanners and phone/tablet cameras scan item, bin, job, lot and serial codes for receiving, issue, count, transfer, pick and output. GS1 data separates identifier, lot and expiry; wrong/stale/held codes are refused with a recoverable reason. **Acceptance evidence:** Scan a receipt and issue/pick it through the shop-floor UI using a hardware scanner and camera; reject a wrong item, held lot and duplicate scan without an extra movement.

- **FLOOR-009 — Zebra and printable labels.** Operators print Zebra ZPL and PDF labels for bins, items, lots, serials, WIP travellers and packages with buyer templates and reprint audit. Codes resolve to authorized live records; label printing alone never posts stock or a shipment. **Acceptance evidence:** Print/read back a Zebra lot label and PDF traveller, reprint with reason and scan on another station; verify no stock change until its authorized movement.


### Stock trace and quality

- **TRACE-001 — Lot and batch genealogy.** From any receipt, output or shipment lot, buyers trace upstream inputs and suppliers and downstream split and merged batches, subcontract work, rework, shipments and customers with quantities and dates. Searches handle a lot spread across locations and consumption across multiple input lots. **Acceptance evidence:** Make one batch from two input lots, split it across shipments and rework one return; query both forward and backward trace and reconcile every quantity.

- **TRACE-002 — Serial-number lifecycle.** Items can require unique serials at receipt/output, issue, assembly, shipment, service and return. Serial-to-assembly/component relationships and warranty/customer history are searchable; duplicate serials and impossible serial quantities are refused. **Acceptance evidence:** Assemble serialized components into two serialized products, ship and return one; trace the exact customer/component and refuse a duplicate or previously shipped serial.

- **TRACE-003 — Expiry and FEFO.** Food, cosmetic and supplement makers record manufacture, expiry/retest and supplier shelf-life dates, storage conditions and customer minimum remaining shelf life. FEFO proposes eligible lots and blocks expired/held stock unless a qualified documented disposition permits it. **Acceptance evidence:** Receive two dated lots, plan and pick FEFO for a customer shelf-life rule, advance past expiry and inspect blocked stock, alerts and an authorized disposition.

- **TRACE-004 — Quality checkpoints and measurements.** Quality staff define receipt, in-process and final inspection plans by SKU and revision with sample size, measurements, tolerance, attachments and qualified signoff. Required failures hold related stock and operations and prevent shipment until authorized release. **Acceptance evidence:** Fail a dimensional in-process check, attach measurements, attempt a downstream start or shipment and see refusal; record an authorized retest and release and preserve both results.

- **TRACE-005 — Nonconformance and disposition.** Quality owns nonconformances with affected quantities, root cause, supplier/customer complaint, corrective action, due date and disposition: hold, reject, rework, return or approved deviation. Closure verifies effectiveness and retains evidence without an agent granting release. **Acceptance evidence:** Open a nonconformance on part of a lot, route rework and supplier action, approve release of only passing units and close after a recorded effectiveness check.

- **TRACE-006 — Recall execution and reconciliation.** Quality can place an immediate scoped hold, expand it using genealogy, identify affected customers/shipments and generate/send approved recall notices with response tracking. Returned, destroyed, unrecovered and cleared quantities reconcile; US food/cosmetic/supplement trace records export for the responsible compliance team. **Acceptance evidence:** Recall one contaminated ingredient spanning two batches/customers; hold unsent goods, send approved notices, record acknowledgements/returns/destruction and export a complete quantity-balanced recall pack.

- **TRACE-007 — Certificates and controlled release records.** Receipts and finished lots retain supplier CoAs, SDSs, test reports, specifications and certificates of conformance with versions, digest and expiry. Customer-required certificates accompany approved shipments, and missing required evidence blocks release. **Acceptance evidence:** Require a CoA and final CoC on one SKU, upload a mismatched lot document and refuse release; approve correct records, ship and export the exact versions with trace.


### Warehouse operations

## Honest boundaries

Larder is a separate manufacturing product. [Pier](https://github.com/just-every/run-pier) owns broader wholesale/distribution, [Pantry](https://github.com/just-every/run-pantry) statutory books, [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit) CRM, [Auth](https://github.com/just-every/run-auth) identity, [Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) cross-tool automation, [Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) team chat and [Chest](https://github.com/just-every/run-chest) document storage. LINK-001/LINK-002 and CONN-008/GOV-003 provide handoffs; shared code does not combine their products. Unowned categories are not absorbed.

QuickBooks Online, Xero or Pantry remain the general ledger and tax/financial statement authority. Payroll, W-2/1099 processing, banking, ACH/card collection and supplier finance belong to Gusto/ADP, the ledger and buyer-authorized payment providers; Larder supplies approved time/cost and bill/invoice references, not a payment institution. US sales tax calculation/exemption integration is US-002; filing/remittance stays with the tax provider/accountant.

Larder does not operate machines, certify safety, grant FDA/GMP approval or replace qualified food/cosmetic/supplement quality staff. TRACE-003 through TRACE-007 deliver expiry, controlled quality records and recalls; deployment-specific electronic-record/signature validation, retention agreements and specialist eQMS/lab connections are implementation work, not grounds to omit these workflows. Supplier/lab documents and qualified decisions remain evidence.

SolidWorks and CAD/CAM tools own geometry/toolpaths; ProNest/TruTops own nesting engines and Datanomix machine observation. CONN-011 through CONN-013 and CAP-008 integrate them. Continuous-process plant control and enterprise chemical formulation are outside the chosen discrete/batch-maker category; batch recipes, yields, co-products, shelf life and trace for the chosen makers are inside it. ShipStation, SPS Commerce and 3PLs operate carrier/EDI/warehouse networks; Larder owns their manufacturing handoffs and reconciliation.

Schedules must be feasible with visible uncertainty, never described as mathematically optimal without evidence. Physical receipt, production, inspection, dispatch and destruction need named human/device evidence; a restore cannot undo a dispatched parcel, supplier order, tax commitment or external journal. US-first does not preclude supplier foreign-currency costs: preserve original currency/rate and USD valuation, while statutory multi-entity/global consolidation belongs to external books.
